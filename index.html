Three small ideas for Interfere's problem page

I read Luke's post on why he started Interfere and studied the product on your site. It already finds the cause fast. These ideas help the same problem make sense to an engineer, a PM and a designer. Try the switches and click the problems.

Why these choices

  • One sentence everyone agrees onEvery problem opens with a plain summary, like "People can't reset their password." Each role starts from the same truth.
  • Same evidence, different first viewThe engineer sees the code change first, the PM sees who is affected, the designer sees the broken step. Nobody has to ask someone else to translate.
  • Show the experience, not only the errorThe step strip shows where people dropped off, so a fix can be judged by whether people get through again.
  • No error is not no problemYour post says a flow can work exactly as specified and still leave people stuck. Those problems get their own label in the inbox.
  • Built on what is already thereThe inbox, properties, findings and code finding stay as they are. Each idea can be switched off.
  • A default, not a modeThe switch here shows the idea. In the product, each person could simply land on the view for their role, and switch only when they need to.
  • Honest numbersEvery number here is an example. In the real product, each one would link back to the sessions and findings behind it.

What I would ask your users first

  1. When a PM opens a problem, what is the first question they need answered?
  2. Do designers want to watch sessions, or see the broken step first?
  3. Which "no error" problems have hurt your customers the most?
